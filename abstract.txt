Title: Modeling Species-Specific Acoustic Responses to the 2024 Total Eclipse

Solar eclipses are a rare natural experiment to study the effect that sudden changes in light have on animal activity, in particular, vocalizations. We used an AI species identifier (BirdNET-Analyzer developed by Cornell Lab of Ornithology) to identify species within audio files to investigate the overall and species-level responses to the eclipse. Through our data exploration, we found that the overall vocalization patterns were different on the day of the eclipse (April 8th) compared to the other days sampled (March 30th - April 16th) over a 1 hr. interval centered at totality. Through our exploratory visualizations, we saw that the overall vocalizations increased during the eclipse (peaking at totality), but specific birds and anurans vocalized differently. We then used a GAMM model to investigate the presence/absence of all species vocalizations using time of day, day, site, and an autoregressive term. We found a difference in vocalization presence during the eclipse compared to the surrounding days, and that time of day was useful in predicting the presence of calls.
