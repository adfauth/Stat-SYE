Title: Modeling Species-Specific Acoustic Responses to the 2024 Total Eclipse

Solar eclipses are a rare natural experiment to investigate the effect that sudden changes in light have on animal activity, in particular, vocalizations. We used an AI species identifier (BirdNET-Analyzer developed by Cornell Lab of Ornithology) to identify species within audio files to investigate the overall and species-level responses to the eclipse. Through our data exploration, we found that, during a 1 hr. interval centered at totality, the overall vocalization pattern was different on the day of the eclipse (April 8th) compared to the other days sampled (March 30th - April 16th). Through our exploratory visualizations, we saw that the overall vocalizations increased during the eclipse, but specific birds and anurans responed differently. We then used a GAMM model to investigate the presence/absence of all species vocalizations incorporating time, day, site, and an autoregressive term. We found a difference in vocalization presence during the eclipse compared to the surrounding days, and that time of day was useful in predicting the presence of calls.
